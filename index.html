Air unit comparison

Hover power against tether length

How it’s calculated
  • The air unit replaces the flight battery. Weight the drone holds in hover = frame + air unit + payload + backup battery + hanging cable (length × cable weight per metre) + tether tension.
  • Hover power uses momentum theory: P = (W·g)1.5 ÷ √(2ρ·N·A) ÷ (FM × η) + avionics + payload power, where A is one rotor’s disc area.
  • For coaxial frames, hover power is multiplied by the coaxial penalty (default 20%) to account for the lower rotor working in the upper rotor’s wake. The rotor count must be even.
  • Maximum tether length is the smallest of three limits: hover power within the air unit rating minus the reserve, total weight within MTOW, and the cable length on the drum (150 m for 3 kW, 100 m for 6 kW).
  • Each air unit pairs with its own ground unit, which holds the tether: the 3 kW air unit with the 3 kW ground unit (150 m of tether), and the 6 kW air unit with the 6.5 kW ground unit (100 m of tether).
  • Power available to the drone is the lower of the air unit rating and the ground unit output. Below 180 V input (the 100–120 VAC option), the 3 kW ground unit is limited to 1.75 kW and the 6.5 kW ground unit to 3.25 kW.
  • The power reserve is 20% with a backup battery and 40% without one, because without a battery the drone has nothing to fall back on in a gust or a tether power loss.
  • Height assumes the cable hangs close to vertical. Wind, drift and cable drag reduce real height and add load, so treat the result as an upper bound.
  • When both units reach a similar height, the lighter 3 kW unit is recommended.
